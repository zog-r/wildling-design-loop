# Copy (Deliver round)

Copy doesn't change the visuals, but it may decide more than anything else whether the page reads as tasteful
or as slop. People are worn out on AI prose and skim past walls of it.

Treat model-written copy the way designers treat Lorem ipsum: it shows the structure and is always a
placeholder. Rewrite every line in one consistent voice.

## What the rewrite does (from the source article's before/after)
The article's example is a database query analyzer. The AI version runs four paragraphs of storytelling:
a scene-setting anecdote, a cute simile, stacked rhetorical fragments ("No X, no Y, no Z"), a repeated
punchline, and a fragment CTA. The human rewrite keeps the same headline and tagline and cuts the body to
two short paragraphs:
1. The pain in one line, then what the product does and for whom, in one plain sentence with the concrete
   specifics (works with any database, flags issues as they appear, points to the exact line of code,
   private).
2. One proof point (time to first result) and a plain CTA.

## Rules
- Shorter and simpler than the draft, always. Cut scene-setting, similes, and clever asides.
- One idea per sentence. Concrete nouns and numbers beat adjectives.
- No stacked fragments ("No agents. No sidecars. No YAML."), no repeated punchlines, no em-dash asides.
- No marketing verbs: elevate, unlock, supercharge, seamless, effortless, empower, revolutionize.
- The CTA says what happens ("Connect your first database for free"), not a slogan.
- Keep every must-keep fact, number, and date verbatim. Keep headings unless they're pure filler.
